    Reading these numbers

    • These are micro-benchmarks of the cell alone, run on shared CI machines. Compare values within one machine and one run.
    • The subjects do not offer the same guarantees. Some hand out borrows and others owned Arcs; some return old values to the writer and others drop them on a reader thread; some let a slow reader block the writer. COMPARISONS.md describes the differences.
    • The writer in “Read beside a writer” publishes continuously, and the readers in “Write” read continuously. Both are far harsher than a typical read-mostly workload.
    • The CI machines have 4 CPUs. Reads without a writer go up to 4 readers; with a writer the sweep stops at 3 so the writer has a CPU of its own.
    • The range drawn around each dot on the log scale is criterion's 95% confidence interval for the median.
    • ◆ and ◇ mark subjects whose readers keep old values alive while idle. It is measured, not declared: the writer publishes a value, one reader reads it and then does nothing, and the writer publishes 100,000 more. ◆: most of those are still allocated afterwards, and would not be had the reader gone away first. ◇: only the value the reader read is still allocated, typically because the reader holds an Arc of it. local-rcu-cached is ◆ by design (call release() before going idle); plain local-rcu keeps nothing.
    • ≡ marks subjects that free old values in batches. Also measured: the writer publishes 100,000 values with no reader anywhere and then stops. A subject is marked when more than a handful of allocations are still alive (at least 8, well short of all of them): they wait for a later write, which may never come. The count is taken again 200 ms later; a subject whose leftovers are gone by then would be marked ↻ instead, for a background task doing the freeing. No subject here has one. basedrop's collector could run on another thread, but this benchmark runs it in the writer after every write.
    • baseline-leak never frees anything. It is the floor, not a contender.
